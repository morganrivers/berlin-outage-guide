Power outage in Berlin: first minutes to first week

Claude Opus 5.5, an AI model, wrote this entire guide. I am not responsible for your safety. Treat it as an unreviewed guide that may be incorrect. Still, it is better than nothing. I also put together a large offline library: the last section shows how to download it, with a script on Linux or by hand.

Offline map: berlin_emergency_map.html shows every Kat-Leuchtturm, hand pump, hospital, fire and police station in Berlin. It is one file that opens in any browser without internet or an app. Save it to your phone now. Press and hold a spot to list the nearest of each.

Written 22 Nov 2026 for a 4th floor flat, two or three adults, radiator heating. Links open the original sources online, or the copy in this repo. The last section shows how to get all of them offline. Grey "Source" lines name the document that backs each step.

Supplies to keep (28 person-days)

A person-day is what one person needs for one day. 28 person-days is 2 adults for 14 days, or 3 adults for about 9 days. BBK advises at least 10 days, and at least 3 days as a start. Source: BBK guide, PDF page 8

  • Drinking water: 2 litres per person per day, so 56 litres in total. Source: BBK guide, PDF page 9
  • Durable food that needs no fridge, for 28 person-days. Source: BBK checklist, PDF page 34
  • Radio (battery, solar or crank), charged power bank, torch and spare batteries, numbers on paper, warm clothes, sleeping bags, cash. Source: BBK checklist, PDF page 34
  • Small solar panel (USB) to recharge the power bank and phone. No source: common practice. BBK lists only a charged power bank.
  • Medicine cabinet: personal medication, painkillers, thermometer, diarrhoea remedies, electrolytes, plasters. Source: BBK checklist, PDF page 35
Numbers and radio (write these on paper too)
  • Fire brigade and ambulance: 112. Police: 110. Source: berlin.de Rufnummern
  • If 112 and 110 do not work: go to the nearest police station, fire station or open Kat-Leuchtturm (list below). Source: BBK guide, PDF page 15; berlin.de Stromausfall
  • Medical on-call service (not an emergency): 116117. Source: 116117.de
  • Poison line Berlin: 030 19240. Source: Giftnotruf Charité
  • Police non-emergency line: 030 4664 4664. Source: berlin.de Rufnummern
  • Stromnetz Berlin power fault line: 0800 211 2525 (free). Source: stromnetz.berlin/kontakt, read on 21 Nov 2026. The site was offline on 22 Nov, so there is no saved copy.
  • Radio (FM): rbb 88.8 on 88.8 MHz, rbb24 Inforadio on 93.1 MHz, radioeins 95.8 MHz. DAB+: channel 7D. A battery, solar or crank radio works when phone networks fail. Source: rbb frequencies; BBK guide, PDF page 15; radio types: BBK checklist, PDF page 34

First minutes

  1. Check if only your flat is dark. Look at the stairwell light and the windows across the street. If only your flat is dark, check your fuse box (Sicherungskasten). No source: common practice.
  2. Use a torch or battery light. Do not heat with candles or a gas stove. Source: FEMA power outage sheet, PDF page 2
  3. Unplug appliances and electronics. A power surge can damage them when the power comes back. Source: FEMA power outage sheet, PDF page 1
  4. Leave one lamp switched on, so you see when power returns. No source: common practice.
  5. Keep the fridge and freezer closed. Source: CDC food safety, PDF page 1
  6. Fill water now, while the tap still runs: bathtub, pots, bottles. Waterworks need electricity; without it the water supply fails. Source: BBK guide, PDF page 6; amounts on BBK guide, PDF page 9
  7. Switch the phone to power saving mode. Send one short message to the people who need to know you are fine. Networks can fail soon after the power. Source: BBK guide, PDF page 14
  8. Turn on the radio for official information. Source: BBK guide, PDF page 15
  9. Do not use the lift. If you are stuck in a lift, press its alarm button. Source: lift use, BBK guide, PDF page 32; alarm button: no source, common practice.

First hour

  1. Expect the radiators to go cold. Central heating needs electricity for its pumps and controls. Source: BBK guide, PDF page 25
  2. Dress warm. Pick one room, close its doors, stay there. Air it briefly (a few minutes, window wide open) a few times a day. Source: BBK guide, PDF page 25; berlin.de Stromausfall
  3. Never use a camping stove, barbecue, generator or gas heater indoors unless it is approved for indoor use. Carbon monoxide kills without warning. Its signs are flu-like symptoms: headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea. If a CO alarm sounds or someone gets these signs near a stove or heater, get everyone out into fresh air and call 112. A battery CO alarm is a good buy. Source: BBK guide, PDF page 24; FEMA power outage sheet, PDF page 2; Wikipedia: Carbon monoxide poisoning
  4. Cooking indoors without power: tea light warmer or fondue set for small amounts. Camping gas stoves: outdoors only. Source: BBK guide, PDF page 24
  5. Get cash if a shop or bank still works. ATMs and card payment fail without power. Source: BBK guide, PDF page 7; berlin.de Stromausfall
  6. Tell your neighbours what you know. Not everyone gets the warnings. Source: BBK guide, PDF page 13
  7. Find your nearest Kat-Leuchtturm in the list below or on the offline map. Note its address on paper. Source: Berlin flyer

First day

  1. Kat-Leuchttürme: they are not open all the time. In a long outage each district decides whether, where and for how long to open them, and the opening is announced separately: listen to the radio. Some listed sites are not ready yet (both in Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg, for example). An open one takes emergency calls and gives news, official advice and information about supplies and help. Charging, medical care, food and beds are not on that list, so do not count on them. Source: berlin.de Kat-Leuchttürme; Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg Kat-Leuchttürme; services: kat_leuchttuerme.csv
  2. Food: a closed fridge keeps food safe for 4 hours. A full closed freezer: 48 hours; half full: 24 hours. Eat the fridge food first. If in doubt, throw it out. Source: CDC food safety, PDF page 1
  3. Toilet: if the drains still work, flush with a bucket of water you do not need for drinking when the cistern stays empty, and flush sparingly. In the January 2026 blackout the sewers kept working, partly on emergency generators. If sewage backs up, the drains stop working, or you are told not to flush, stop using the toilet. Seal it shut. Use a bucket with a plastic bag and a lid. Put dry material in the bottom (sawdust, newspaper, leaves). Cover each use with sawdust, ash or similar. Use a second bucket for urine if you can. Source: flushing with a bucket: no source, common practice; January 2026: rbb24, 16:53 entry; buckets: Wikipedia: Bucket toilet; WREMO emergency toilets; Wikipedia: Emergency sanitation
  4. Water: drink your stored water first. If the taps are dry, Berlin has street hand pumps (Notbrunnen), but about 30% of them do not work (1,394 of 1,976 working), and Berliner Wasserbetriebe says they do not deliver drinking water. Follow the authorities' instructions on which pumps to use and how to treat the water. Boiling clear water for 1 minute kills germs; disinfecting tablets used as directed kill most of them. Neither removes chemicals: if the water may be chemically contaminated, do not drink it. The pumps are on the offline map, and 1982 are in notbrunnen_hand_pumps.gpx for Organic Maps or OsmAnd. Source: pumps working and water quality: Berliner Wasserbetriebe Notbrunnen; boiling, tablets and chemicals: CDC Make water safe, PDF page 1; pump locations from OpenStreetMap, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL (CSV)
  5. Night: light and warmth. Sleep in clothes and a sleeping bag in your warm room. Know the signs of hypothermia (shivering, then confusion). Source: Where There Is No Doctor, emergencies caused by cold; Wikipedia: Hypothermia
  6. Fire: leave the room, close the door, leave the building, call 112. Source: BBK guide, PDF page 26

First week

  1. Drink at least 2 litres per person per day (0.5 litres of that for cooking). For 2 adults the 56 litre stock lasts 14 days; for 3 adults it lasts about 9 days. Count what is left on day 3 and day 7. Source: BBK guide, PDF page 9
  2. Diarrhoea: drink rehydration drink. 1 litre clean water, half a level teaspoon of salt, 8 level teaspoons of sugar. It must taste less salty than tears. Source: Where There Is No Doctor, rehydration drink
  3. Stress and fear in you or others: the WHO guide shows how to help people calmly. Source: WHO Psychological first aid
  4. Chemical or gas release outside: go inside, close windows and doors, go to upper floors. Radiation or explosions: go low, into inner rooms or the cellar, away from windows. Source: BBK guide, PDF page 30; BBK guide, PDF page 32
  5. For anything else, use the offline library (last section). After one week you are in a different situation; this guide stops here.

The offline library

This guide links to its sources online. Files that may be shared are in the repo. To have every source offline, plus offline wikis with a reader, use the script or the steps by hand.

With the script (Linux)

  1. Install git, curl and python3. Chrome or Chromium saves one web page as PDF.
  2. Run git clone https://github.com/morganrivers/berlin-outage-guide
  3. Run berlin-outage-guide/download.sh. It downloads about 3.8 GB into the cloned folder. To use another folder, give it as the first argument.
